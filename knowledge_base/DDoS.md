# DDoS

## Executive Summary
Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks flood a target with high-volume traffic from multiple distributed sources, aiming to exhaust network or application resources and disrupt availability.

## Description
Coordinated traffic, often generated by a botnet, is directed at a target simultaneously from many hosts, overwhelming bandwidth, connection tables, or application processing capacity. Attacks may be volumetric, protocol-based, or application-layer.

## Objectives
Disrupt service availability, cause financial or reputational damage, or serve as a distraction for concurrent intrusion attempts.

## Attack Lifecycle
Botnet assembly/rental → target selection → coordinated attack launch across distributed sources → sustained flooding → potential attack rotation across vectors → cessation or mitigation.

## Typical Network Behaviour
- Traffic pattern: sudden, sustained high-volume influx from numerous source IPs.
- Packet characteristics: often uniform or minimal packet sizes optimized for volume over payload.
- Communication behavior: many-to-one traffic convergence on a single target.
- Session behavior: incomplete or abnormal connection sequences at scale.
- Protocol usage: varies — TCP SYN floods, UDP floods, or HTTP request floods.
- Timing: near-continuous, high-frequency packet arrival with minimal inter-arrival delay.
- Flow characteristics: highly asymmetric flows, overwhelming forward volume with minimal legitimate backward exchange.

## Indicators of Compromise (IoCs)
- Network: sudden bandwidth spikes, high connection rates from geographically diverse IPs.
- Host: resource exhaustion (CPU, memory, connection tables) on target servers.
- Behavioral: sustained abnormal traffic volume disproportionate to normal usage patterns.
- Log-based: load balancer/firewall logs showing mass connection attempts within seconds.

## MITRE ATT&CK Mapping
- Tactic: Impact — Technique: Network Denial of Service (T1498). Directly applicable as the technique explicitly covers volumetric network flooding to degrade availability.

## Detection Methods
Traffic volume anomaly detection, IDS/IPS rate-based rules, flow-based statistical analysis (NetFlow/sFlow), SIEM correlation of multi-source spikes, and ML models trained on packet rate and flow symmetry deviations.

## Explainable AI Feature Interpretation
`Flow Bytes/s` and `Flow Packets/s` are typically extreme, reflecting the volumetric nature of the attack. `Total Length of Fwd Packets` and `act_data_pkt_fwd` often spike sharply due to sheer packet volume. `Max Packet Length` and `Average Packet Size` may show unusual uniformity if attack tools generate standardized packets. `Bwd Packet Length Mean`/`Std` can be minimal since target responses are limited or absent under load, reflecting the overwhelming forward-dominant flow characteristic of DDoS.

## Severity Assessment
- Confidentiality Impact: None to Low
- Integrity Impact: Low
- Availability Impact: Critical
- Overall Severity: Critical
- Business Impact: Service outage, SLA violations, revenue loss, reputational harm.

## Recommended Mitigation
DDoS scrubbing/mitigation services, rate limiting, anycast distribution, upstream ISP filtering, and network segmentation to isolate critical assets.

## Incident Response
Preparation (mitigation service contracts, capacity planning) → Detection (volume anomaly alerts) → Containment (traffic scrubbing, blackholing malicious ranges) → Eradication (source mitigation) → Recovery (service restoration) → Lessons Learned (capacity and mitigation review).

## SOC Analyst Investigation Guide
Confirm traffic source diversity, correlate with upstream provider alerts, review target resource utilization metrics, and determine attack vector (volumetric vs. protocol vs. application-layer) to guide mitigation.

## Common False Positives
Legitimate traffic surges (flash crowds, viral content, marketing campaigns) can resemble DDoS; validate against source diversity and legitimacy of request patterns.

## AI Incident Report Context
The flow exhibits extreme packet and byte rate characteristics consistent with a volumetric denial-of-service attack, indicating a coordinated attempt to exhaust target network or application resources.
